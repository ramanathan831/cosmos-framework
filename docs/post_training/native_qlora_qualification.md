# Native QLoRA qualification checklist

**The native QLoRA path is implemented and experimentally measured, but it is not release-qualified.** The existing trained NF4 model's supervised-token NLL was 4.18% higher than the matched BF16 control on the exposed WTS diagnostic, exceeding the unchanged 2% margin. A subsequent data audit also found related recording families across the original training and validation splits. Neither faster preprocessing nor new configuration acceptance resolves these quality requirements.

This checklist reviews implementation commit `55e7f14dfeca84002d0324108808a0e682579842`. Historical full-training evidence belongs to the earlier sealed `c5b8d49` and `a406ea1` runtimes. The [continuation kernel gate](evidence/native_qlora_kernel_gate_55e7f14.json) passed all 14 CUDA cases and the existing higher-precision native CPU regression for the exact `55e7f14` wheel, without skips. All eight development experiments have their own [completed training and evaluation evidence](evidence/native_qlora_development_candidates_55e7f14.json). Additional workload settings and confirmation seeds still require their own receipts; historical evidence does not automatically qualify a new build. See the [native training guide](native_qlora.md), [original design](qlora_native_vlm_design.md), and [qualification tool instructions](../../tools/native_qlora_qualification/README.md).

The [continuation audit evidence](evidence/native_qlora_qualification_audit_20261030.json) records the four-arm reanalysis, per-example differences and uncertainty, source metadata and split audit, sealed experiment criteria, and completed CPU workload shapes. It does not claim completion of the new GPU runs.

The [new build-preservation check](evidence/native_qlora_cross_build_55e7f14.json) reproduced the archived four-update NF4 and BF16 states from fresh training with the original inputs. Adapter and optimizer payloads, trainer state and data cursor were byte-identical. DCP index serialization differed, while decoded entries matched; model metadata differed only in the two declared code/wheel identity fields. This qualifies the timing counters for that diagnostic trajectory. It does not establish a new 300-update result or permit cross-build production resume.

The [completed development lifecycle checks](evidence/native_qlora_development_lifecycles_55e7f14.json) cover all eight seed 42 settings: both precision arms at rank 16/alpha 32 with learning rates `5e-5`, `1e-4` and `2e-4`, plus rank 32/alpha 64 at `1e-4`. Each reference/resume comparison used four updates, microbatch one with accumulation eight, full activation checkpointing and FP32 adapter arithmetic. These checks used the unchanged `55e7f14` wheel and sealed `941b645` qualification workflow.

All 120 stages completed as expected, including 16 deliberate rank crashes after committed update two or during accumulation for update three. Partial-accumulation publication was refused. Recovery from each run's own committed checkpoint matched uninterrupted model/optimizer state and trainer/RNG/data controls at updates three and four, excluding only destination-specific publication fields. Frozen-base integrity and exactly 504 FP32 adapter tensors in optimizer groups were verified. Fresh DCP and portable reloads matched controlled text, image and video logits/loss with zero tolerance, with actual adapter updates and media-conditioning effects. This establishes the stated four-update recovery behavior; the separate completed 300-update experiments below do not establish recovery from an interruption at update 300.

The separate [completed long-sequence recovery evidence](evidence/native_qlora_long_recovery_55e7f14.json) extends those correctness checks to the prepared `long4k_video8` workload at rank 16/alpha 32, microbatch one with accumulation eight and full activation checkpointing. Both BF16 and NF4 passed all 15 stages, including their own committed-update and mid-accumulation interruptions, exact next-update-three and final-update-four comparisons, and fresh text/image/video DCP and portable reloads. Each uninterrupted four-update trajectory processed 32 training examples and 80,777 supervised tokens. The actual video reload probe contained 4,075 tokens, including 2,623 supervised tokens; complete prepared-row shapes and native counters remain separate evidence scopes.

This recovery-only allocation completed in 2,858 seconds (0.794 GPU-hours). It used the unchanged `55e7f14` native package and `941b645` lifecycle code, and passed its final placement check. It does not repair the original long-workload host-memory gaps, qualify a memory budget, establish WTS task quality or demonstrate recovery at update 20 or 300. The long targets are synthetic text paired with real video.

In idealized weight notation, training uses `dequantize(Q(W)) + ΔW`. Applying the same adapter to the original BF16 `W` instead yields `W + ΔW`, a different numerical model. Exporting dequantized quantized weights plus the adapter is also subject to arithmetic/rounding changes, and subsequent requantization changes the model again. None of these operations inherits the same-quantized-base reload result. Universal in-place four-bit merging and serving-backend compatibility are not promised.
