# Add shared 8-bit AdamW with native GR00T FSDP2 master-weight recovery

Native BF16 action-policy training needs an explicit FP32 update owner before it can safely use quantized optimizer moments. This change extends the existing optional bitsandbytes 0.49.2 backend with tracked FP32 masters for ordinary parameters and complete one-dimensional FSDP2 shards. It retains model parameter identity, trainable groups, native compute precision, clipping and legacy defaults. Model weights are not quantized.

SFT keeps its existing ordinary-FP32 implementation. A native four-GPU GR00T/LIBERO recipe now exercises the shared factory through Joint PPO, actor/value groups, rollout policy synchronization, real simulator inputs and completed-window checkpoint recovery. AlpaGym construction also reaches the shared factory after model setup and preserves its epsilon 1e-6, betas, decay, LR/warmup and clipping. Alpamayo integration remains **incomplete**: the required external `alpagym_runtime.posttrain` APIs, approved resolved driving configuration and simulator/scenarios are unavailable. Construction tests do not qualify that workflow.

Checkpoints preserve rank-owned masters, lazy state, uint8 moments, scales/maps and logical groups. An opaque byte-tensor envelope avoids large-object serialization limits while retaining older format readers. Restore stages identity checks before mutation, converges failures across ranks and rolls back failed application. Collector receipts bind the data boundary to the restored generation. Fresh-process evaluation verifies provenance before publishing scores. Physical environments reset at the declared completed-window boundary; identical future simulator trajectories are not promised. A pressure-recovery OOM exposed backend classes retaining retired master buffers. Backend types are now cached independently of per-instance parameters; a fail-before/pass-after ownership test and an immediate-release CUDA recovery gate cover that lifecycle.

Captured stale Lustre timestamps exposed a publication failure that server-synchronized metadata alone did not fix. Publication now independently hashes every regular payload on every rank, detects mutation during reads and rejects unread symlink subtrees. Existing load/copy provenance remains compatible. This adds measured I/O; it is a correctness fix, not a speed claim. Pending-validation recovery on the tested mount uses the original output root because atomic cross-directory no-replace promotion is unavailable.

Completed validation includes:

- Two A100 ranks: fused, nonpaged and paged reference/resume pairs, with exact every-update continuation 4→8; accumulation, clipping, empty/unused shards, nonfinite rejection, rank failures, corruption, rollback and managed-allocation release.
- Native four-GPU GR00T: all three optimizers train and publish checkpoints. Nonpaged and paged fresh reload and continuation 4→8 pass. Fused and nonpaged continuation 40→80 pass, including exact restored learner/data boundaries and all 40 subsequent finite updates. The paged55-GiB pressure reference32, fresh reload16 and corrected16→32 continuation pass; corrected nonpaged55 continuation passes updates17–19 then OOMs at update20. That fixed-pressure row remains failed; the instrumented migration diagnostic stops after three updates with a backward stall. Its partial rank1 trace demonstrates repeated eviction/prefetch but does not qualify migration overhead or sustained speed.
- The longer nonpaged run completes 80 updates and checkpoint 40/80 publication. Fresh final-checkpoint reloads reproduce every saved learner-state field in both optimizer arms. The fused reference completes training/evaluation but fails final publication; corrected pending-validation recovery publishes it with zero new updates. All failures remain recorded and charged.
- Moments plus metadata fall from 12,998,755,840 to 3,304,438,792 bytes: **74.5788% less**. Both controls also require 6,499,373,572 bytes of FP32 masters; including them gives **49.7192% less**, below a 60% requirement applied to all persistent optimizer-owned tensors. Neither percentage measures total device memory.
- Three paired CPU trials on an actual native rank checkpoint show a shared encoding roundtrip improvement of **3.98× [3.50, 4.45]**, with mean process peak PSS 25.44→17.96 GiB. This is a prepared CPU checkpoint result, not complete-training speed.

The product quality and speed requirements remain open. At 80 updates, native success is 95/100 fused and 91/100 nonpaged, versus 97/100 initially. Both adaptation screens fail; nonpaged also fails the locked success-loss and episode-step guards. No final superiority study was admitted. Exact-state reload scores vary under closed-loop execution; original endpoint scores remain authoritative. One training seed and these exposed cases cannot establish recipe-level confidence bounds. There is no successful matched end-to-end delivery comparison across the corrected publication implementation.

Observed nonpaged/fused device peaks are 26.19/28.89 GiB and full-launcher host PSS peaks are 69.28/87.90 GiB. These are individual observations with different publication implementations, not paired statistical memory or speed claims. Historical WTS state savings remain 74.6061%; its quality uncertainty, equal-resource quality and prepared/cold speed requirements are unchanged and unmet.
